# Lead notification email by rental space

Current reference: [Managing Tenant Lead Notifications](https://support.furnishedfinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/47737008712859-Managing-Tenant-Lead-Notifications) and [What Notifications Can I Turn On or Off?](https://support.furnishedfinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/55708926082203-What-Notifications-Can-I-Turn-On-or-Off), checked October 25, 2026. Furnished Finder separates property notification channels from owner-wide unmatched intake. This batch implements the email portion; SMS/push and other notification types remain open.

Owners use Tenant Housing Requests → Lead preferences → Lead notification email. Account default applies to rental spaces without their own setting. Each space can inherit, turn email on or turn email off. The property picker pages all owned spaces, including drafts and hidden rentals. Changing settings never edits the listing revision, approval, calendar or publication state. Overrides are private rows bound to both the listing and its current owner, so another owner does not inherit a previous owner's override.

Lead intake stays independent. Muting email leaves requests and matching spaces available in the inbox. Global unmatched intake and the traveler's explicit alternative-offer consent still govern partial matches. A fresh environment-matched provider-confirmed notification address is required to enable and send email; mutable profile addresses are never used. An inherited or saved on setting does not bypass expired confirmation.

One owner/request delivery produces at most one provider attempt if any currently eligible matched rental space has email on. A 25-candidate paged selection checks current publication, paid access, requested area, dates/calendars, ownership, blocks, traveler scope, owner unmatched intake and effective property email settings. The atomic prepare step repeats those checks immediately before claiming a provider attempt. A muted or no-longer-eligible selected space triggers a fenced reselection; stale jobs cannot overwrite newer work. Changes after a provider attempt starts cannot recall that email.

Every queued selection has a generation and timestamp. Fifteen-minute recovery requeues stale unattempted selections and pre-upgrade queued work. An already-attempted stale delivery becomes unknown rather than being submitted again. Resend uses a stable per-delivery idempotency key. Only a provider ID records sent; explicit rejection records failed, while transport/application uncertainty or missing acceptance ID records unknown. Failed/unknown attempts require operational reconciliation; there is no automatic resend. Editing a preference alone does not email historical requests. An eligible later native matching scan may queue an existing unattempted suppressed/unconfigured delivery; a sent or attempted delivery never queues again.

Evidence: 8 additional meaningful native-function cases in `tests/housing-requests.test.ts`, including paging, ownership transfer, current proof, inbox retention, reselection, generation fences, concurrent preparation, recovery and provider response distinctions. The twentieth guarded local flow (`scripts/local-lead-email-smoke.mjs`) uses privileged synthetic identity/proof only on loopback with synthetic billing and real email disabled; it verifies native property override selection, mute recheck, inbox retention, inheritance and unchanged listing revision, then closes/hides its fixtures and removes confirmation. Receipt: `docs/evidence/local-lead-email-smoke.json`.

Real inbox delivery, actual provider acceptance, hosted multi-account sends, SMS/push and whole-product notification acceptance remain open. Convex remains the backend; no production configuration or real customer records are changed.
